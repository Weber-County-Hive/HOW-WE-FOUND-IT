HOW WE FOUND IT · RECORDS GUIDE · HW 100.00

Where to look: the public records we use most.

What each record shows, and a direct link to it. Anyone can use them.

Posted Nov. 18, 2026 · Updated Nov. 18, 2026

State campaign money reports

What it shows: Money in and out for state candidates, parties and PACs, plus lobbyists and their clients.

Go to: disclosures.utah.gov

Tip: Advanced Search lets you find every payment to one company, like a campaign vendor. You can download a whole report as a spreadsheet file. The site asks you to prove you are a person before it searches.

State business records

What it shows: Who owns or runs a company or nonprofit, its address, when it formed, and whether it is still active.

Go to: Utah Division of Corporations business search

Tip: Scroll to "Principal Information" at the bottom of a record. That is where members, managers and directors are listed.

County candidate reports

What it shows: Money reports for county races, like a county commission seat. These are filed with the county, not the state.

Go to: Utah County financial disclosures (each county posts its own)

Tip: Some reports are handwritten. Read every page. A total can hide what is in the lines.

Federal election filings (FEC)

What it shows: Federal committees, including each state party's federal account: addresses, treasurers and money reports.

Go to: fec.gov/data

Tip: Form 1 is a committee's sign-up form. It lists the address, email and treasurer.

Party rules and handbooks

What it shows: How a party says its programs work, like the Utah GOP's postage program for candidate mailers.

Go to: Utah GOP County Leadership Handbook

Tip: Compare what the rules say with what the money reports show.
